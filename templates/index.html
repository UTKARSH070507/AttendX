Validation & Integrity Enforcement

  • Strict Type Coercion: Values for attendance and marks are converted to floating-point numbers. Non-numeric entries are quarantined.
  • Range Checking: Attendance must satisfy $0 \le x \le 100$ and marks must satisfy $0 \le y \le 100$. Out-of-bounds rows are rejected.
  • Deduplication: Duplicate roll numbers already registered in the system will not overwrite existing student data.
  • Non-Destructive Parsing: Malformed rows are explicitly rejected with line numbers, while all valid rows are safely committed.

Research Hypotheses Formulation

Research Question 1: Association

$H_0$: The population linear correlation between attendance and examination marks is zero ($\rho = 0$).
$H_1$: The population linear correlation between attendance and examination marks is nonzero ($\rho \ne 0$).

Two-Sided Test (α = 0.05)
Research Question 2: Group Differences

$H_0$: Population mean marks are equal across all attendance tiers ($\mu_{\text{Low}} = \mu_{\text{Med}} = \mu_{\text{High}}$).
$H_1$: At least one attendance group exhibits a statistically distinct mean examination mark.

One-Way ANOVA / Welch's Test

Methodological Clarification & Causality Boundaries

Association vs. Causation: A statistically significant correlation or ANOVA result demonstrates an empirical association, but does not establish that higher attendance directly causes improved examination marks. Confounding factors—such as prior subject preparation, study hours, socioeconomic factors, and classroom engagement—also influence performance.

Project Overview & Research Methodology

Academic framework, problem statement, and mathematical testing procedures

Project Objective

The primary aim of the Student Attendance vs Academic Performance Analysis System (AttendX) is to empirically evaluate whether student class attendance percentage is significantly correlated with examination performance, and whether students belonging to distinct attendance tiers (Low, Medium, High) exhibit statistically significant variances in their examination marks.

Hypothesis 1: Linear Association

Investigated via Pearson's product-moment correlation coefficient ($r$) and Spearman's rank correlation ($\rho$). Evaluated at significance level $\alpha = 0.05$.

Hypothesis 2: Multi-Group Variance

Investigated using One-Way Analysis of Variance (ANOVA). If homogeneity of variance is violated (tested via Levene's test), Welch's ANOVA is applied followed by Games-Howell post-hoc testing.
